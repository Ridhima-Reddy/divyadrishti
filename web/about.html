Ask, measure, profile, then an optometrist decides.

  1. Ask

    One question at a time, in the patient’s own words.

    The app listens until the patient pauses, understands native number words and code-mixed speech like “sugar irukku”, and asks for a tap when it is unsure. Questions that cannot change this patient’s outcome are never asked, and each one says why it is.

  2. Measure

    Then it measures what a questionnaire would guess.

    All five checks of the ZEISS Online Vision Screening, plus duochrome and an N-chart in the patient’s language. The front camera measures reading distance, focusing and convergence near point, and PD; the rear camera reads the power of the glasses the patient already wears.

  3. Profile

    A functional vision profile, not a single number.

    Hours at near, arm’s length and distance map to the Balanced, Intermediate and Near design options that ZEISS individual progressive lenses already take. The reading add is estimated at the distance she actually reads: at a measured 29 cm that is +1.75 D, not the +1.00 a table gives at 40 cm.

  4. Decide

    A remote optometrist signs off every case.

    Cases are encrypted on the shop’s device and sent when signal returns. Optometrists review them against a deadline set by the most urgent finding, sign off or redirect, and send a PIN-protected referral letter to the hospital. Every case exports as a FHIR R4 bundle.

Twelve tests, set up the way an optometrist would.

A phone screen is too small and too close for accurate charts, so the chart tests run on the shop’s test screen: a laptop, monitor or TV at 3 or 6 m, letters at their true size. The patient answers on their own phone. The camera handles what a camera measures best. Every test is always on the list; the engine marks the ones that matter for this patient, and says why.

Only emergencies go red.

One symptom per question, each about something new or sudden, with follow-ups only after a yes. Every finding gets the slowest safe time to care, so a hospital sees the patients who need it today.

  1. EmergencySame day

    Chemical or high-speed injury, a fixed curtain, sudden loss within two days, angle-closure signs, contact-lens keratitis, double vision with neurological signs.

  2. Urgent18 hours to 2 weeks

    A crop-leaf scratch, new floaters, a painful red eye, new Amsler distortion, a white pupil glow.

  3. Soon2 to 6 weeks

    Possible cataract affecting vision, vision worse than 6/60 in an eye, an overdue diabetic retina check.

  4. RoutineOptometrist sign-off

    Glasses can proceed after review; a routine referral where needed.

  5. NoteFor the record

    Recorded for the optometrist; no referral.

Every rule, its time to care and its source is open: in the optical store app, in the optometrist portal and in the project documentation.

  • Files travel as expiring, encrypted links. Patient files are shared as end-to-end encrypted links with the key in the link’s fragment, an optional PIN said aloud, and a QR code.

Questions people ask.

Is this a diagnosis?

No. It is a screening and triage tool. An optometrist reviews every case and confirms every power, and anything urgent goes to an eye hospital with a stated time to care.

Does the patient need to install anything?

No. The shop runs the interview. For the chart tests, the patient scans a QR code on the shop’s test screen and answers on their own phone in the browser; the code stops working when their tests end. The patient app is a website too: signing in with the same mobile number shows every visit, prescription and glasses order from network shops. Results can also go by SMS to any phone.

Which languages does it speak?

English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi and Kannada, written the way people speak: native number words, code-mixed answers like “sugar irukku”, and spoken prescriptions.

Who decides what is an emergency?

Forty-three graded rules with published sources set the tier and the time to care; only emergencies go to hospital today. The reviewing optometrists can change a rule’s tier and time from the portal; the check that decides when a rule fires stays fixed.

Can a shop work with more than one optometrist?

Yes. A shop can link a primary optometrist, tele-optometrists and visiting or referral ophthalmologists, and each of them can work with many shops. Cases reach the linked professionals, and the shop books their time slots from the app.

How do you know an optometrist is who they say?

Optometrists and ophthalmologists register with their council and registration number. The network admin verifies them before they can sign off a case; until then they can only look around.

Where is the patient’s data kept?

On the shop’s device, encrypted with a PIN. The server keeps cases under a code, with names sealed so that only the optometrist hub can open them. The ZEISS dashboard sees only counts and rates.
